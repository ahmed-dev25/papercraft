# Validation plan

> What we are proving before scaling to two films a week. This is about craft, speed and a baseline -
> not a growth forecast. Growth needs eight to ten films.

## What is being validated

1. **The look holds at 100%** on a phone, not just in a contact sheet.
2. **The format works without a voice or music** - the images and lines carry 90 seconds.
3. **One film takes <= 1 day of human time**, including the gates.
4. **The last line lands** - a viewer can repeat it or feel it without being told what to feel.
5. **We have a retention and click baseline** to improve against.

## Three gates (no film skips one)

### Gate 1 - script

- Read it aloud at a calm pace; it must be 75-100 s.
- Apply the three-question test: filmable, consistent metaphor, last line adds without explaining.
- A stranger reads it and says what the metaphor is. If they cannot, rewrite.
- Any sentence that needs a visual explanation is not finished.

**Pass:** the reader can say "it's about staying for a light that doesn't care" without the script
saying it.

### Gate 2 - animatic

- 8 stills, one per beat, plus 10 s of motion for the hardest beat.
- Watch at phone size, sound off first, then on.
- Does the turn read with the text covered? If not, the shot is wrong; do not fix it with text.
- Text: 2 lines max, 3 s minimum on screen, never over busy art.

**Pass:** the story is legible with the text off, and the text adds a layer rather than carrying the
plot.

### Gate 3 - final

- Watch at 1080p on a phone at arm's length, then on a laptop.
- Sound off first (most feed views are muted), then on.
- Three people who do not know the project. Ask, in this order:
  1. What happened?
  2. What did it make you feel?
  3. Would you send it to someone? Who?
- Record their words verbatim; do not argue with them.

**Pass:** at least two of three can retell the turn, and one names a person they would send it to.

## Metrics after publishing

| Metric | Starting target | If below |
|---|---|---|
| 15 s retention | > 70% | the first two seconds are wrong; cold-open on the image, not a title |
| 50% retention | > 50% | the middle sags; cut a beat, try 60-75 s |
| Average view duration | > 55% | pacing or text density |
| Thumbnail CTR | > 4% | title/thumbnail problem, not the film |
| Shares + saves | > 1% of views | the ending is not landing; rewrite the last line |
| Comments quoting the line | >= 1 in the first 20 | the film is admired, not felt |

These are starting points. The first three films establish the baseline; the next five test changes
against it, one variable at a time.

Publish weekly on the same day and time. Do not publish two in a day; the algorithm and the audience
both prefer a rhythm.

## Pivot rules

- **Three films in a row under 50% at 15 s** -> the opening is wrong. Test starting on the strongest
  image with the first line already on screen.
- **50% retention under 35%** -> cut a beat. 75 s is not a failure; 90 s that sags is.
- **CTR under 2.5%** -> change the thumbnail and title only. Do not re-edit the film.
- **Good retention, no shares** -> the last line is explaining instead of opening. Rewrite it.
- **Production over 1.5 days** -> reduce scope (one set, six beats), not quality.

## What "validated" means

- Three films shipped, each in <= 1 day of human time.
- The look is consistent and holds at 100% on a phone.
- At least one unprompted viewer quotes the last line.
- A baseline exists for retention, AVD and CTR.
- The render is reproducible: a re-render of one beat matches the previous one.

Only then scale to two films a week, and only by batching two plans and two renders - never by
rushing the gates.

## The growth frame

The look wins the first three seconds. The writing wins the next sixty. The title and thumbnail win the
click. Shorts win the discovery. A channel grows on a consistent promise plus iteration, not one
perfect film. Plan for six to twelve months, not six weeks.
