# 003 - The Wall With a Door In It

**Logline.** A wall keeps the world out for years, until a crack reveals that it was built around a
door - and someone has been knocking the whole time.

**Metaphor.** Wall = self-protection, the story that closeness is dangerous. Door = the capacity for
connection that was always there. Knocking = the people who kept reaching. Layers = years of armour.

**Set.** A cross-section of a paper room: a tall layered wall down the middle, wallpaper strips, a
chair, a small lamp, a coat on a hook, dust. The door is hidden inside the wall. No character on
screen; the wall is the protagonist.

**Cast.** The wall, the door, and a knock (sound only).

**Runtime.** 84 s, 8 beats.

| # | Time | Shot | Text | Visual |

| 1 | 0:00-0:12 | establish | The wall had been in the room longer than the room remembered. | Wide, still. The wall fills the frame; the room is dim and tidy around it. |

| 7 | 1:02-1:12 | approach | The wall had been built around it. | The wall folds down in panels; the door stands alone in the room. |

**Last line.** *Someone had been knocking the whole time.* Not advice - a fact the viewer feels as an
invitation to open the door.

**Notes.**
- The wall is not a villain. It was built for a reason. Show the reason in beat 2 without explaining
  it: the radio, the lamp, the coat are all things that once hurt or asked too much.
- The knocking must be quiet and regular, under the music-less room tone, from beat 1 onward - the
  audience should hear it before the film names it. On a second watch, it is obvious.
- The fold in beats 6-7 is the biggest paper moment in the first three films: wallpaper strips turn
  like hinge panels, the door is a layered card with a real knob. Keep it slow.
- No face, no figure. The room is the character's interior.

**What it is not.** Not "let people in" as a slogan. Not a film about a specific trauma. The wall is
universal; the door is the point.
