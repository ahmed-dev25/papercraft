# 002 - The Boat That Held the Rope

**Logline.** A boat stays in the harbour out of loyalty, until it discovers that the rope was never
holding it - it was holding the rope.

**Metaphor.** Harbour = comfort and belonging. Rope = obligation, guilt, the story that leaving would
be betrayal. Sea = the life not lived yet. The boat = the self that stays.

**Set.** A paper harbour at dusk: layered water bands, a jetty, a cleat, a low lighthouse, three small
boats in the distance, a folded paper boat at the jetty, one rope. Warm dusk key, cool water shadow.

**Cast.** The boat. The other boats are small background cards, never characters.

**Runtime.** 86 s, 8 beats.

| # | Time | Shot | Text | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00-0:12 | establish | The boat had been in the harbour longer than anyone remembered. | Wide, dusk. The boat tied to the jetty; the water breathes; the lighthouse begins to turn. |
| 2 | 0:12-0:22 | closeup | Every morning, the others left without it. | Three small boats slip past the jetty and out. The boat watches; its reflection moves, it does not. |
| 3 | 0:22-0:33 | approach | It always found a reason to stay. | The boat tugs once. The rope holds. A loose plank knocks; it settles. |
| 4 | 0:33-0:43 | reveal | The harbour was safe. The harbour was small. | Camera pulls back: the harbour walls close the frame; the sea is a thin bright line beyond. |
| 5 | 0:43-0:54 | closeup | One night, the rope came loose. | Close on the knot at the jetty. It slips, falls, and lies on the boards. |
| 6 | 0:54-1:03 | hold | The boat did not move. | The boat sits free. The water moves; the boat does not. Three seconds of stillness. |
| 7 | 1:03-1:14 | fold | It had been holding the rope. | The camera turns to the boat's cleat: the rope is knotted there. The jetty end was never tied. The boat was the one holding on. |
| 8 | 1:14-1:26 | retreat | You can go, and still come home. | The boat drifts out past the jetty; the harbour lights stay behind; the rope trails in the water. |

**Last line.** *You can go, and still come home.* An invitation, and the answer to the fear the film
has been building.

**Notes.**
- The reveal in beat 7 must be visual first, text second: show the knot on the boat's cleat and the
  loose jetty end, then let the line land.
- The harbour must look loved, not like a prison. Warm windows, a lamp, a rope coil. The sadness is
  that it is small, not that it is bad.
- The boat has no face. Its want is in the tilt, the tug, the stillness.
- The lighthouse beam is a slow sweep, never a flash.

**What it is not.** Not "cut off your family". Not "comfort is bad". The harbour is good; it is just
not the whole sea.
